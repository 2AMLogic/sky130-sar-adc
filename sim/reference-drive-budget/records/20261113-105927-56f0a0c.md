## Is `less than 5 ohms` in series inside the tolerance?

- `5` Ohm is a SIMULATED point of this sweep at 9/9 corners -- not an interpolation between swept values.
- Worst captured-code movement from the same corner's own `R_ref = 0` control, over those corners and all 5 inputs: **0 LSB**.
- On that evidence a series resistance below `5` Ohm on each reference pad is INSIDE the tolerance this campaign measures -- for a pure series resistance, at this conversion rate, with nothing else on the net.

**This says nothing about a shared analog bus.** The testbench models two resistors. It does not model the other loading such a bus would carry (the other projects sharing it, their switching activity, or any high-frequency behaviour), nor bus/pad capacitance, nor inductance, nor a decoupling network. A bus whose switch resistance happens to be under 5 Ohm is not thereby shown to be usable here; only the series-resistance term of it is addressed.

## What this testbench does not model

- **Series resistance only.** Two resistors, one per reference pad, same value. No bond or trace inductance, no pad or bus capacitance, no decoupling network, no on-chip reference buffer.
- **Nothing else on the net.** No other load, no other project's switching activity, no high-frequency behaviour of a shared bus.
- **`VCM` and the supplies stay ideal at the die**, so the reference is the only non-ideal drive in the deck.
- **DC inputs.** 5 DC differential levels, one conversion each, plus a discarded start-up conversion -- not a dynamic input.
- **One conversion rate.** `f_clk = 12` MHz (DR-006 worst case), `f_s = f_clk/12 = 1` MS/s. A slower clock gives the reference longer to settle and would move these budgets up.
- **`R_ref = 0` is ngspice's zero-resistor floor** (1e-12 Ohm, rewritten with a warning), not a removed element.
