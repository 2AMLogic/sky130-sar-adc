Read every absolute extracted-side voltage below with that in mind: each carries a fraction of the substrate motion through its own C-to-vsubs term. The schematic leg has no substrate net at all, so this term appears wholesale in the delta rows.

## Schematic-vs-extracted transfer delta (per corner)

Both PEX legs use 1.8 V highs for pulsed SEL and reset, and a 0.9 V reset-switch threshold. The transfer campaign uses VDD and VDD/2. The PEX pulse highs are 0.18 V higher at 1.62 V and 0.18 V lower at 1.98 V, increasing and decreasing the initial NMOS gate drive, respectively. The reset threshold differs by +0.09 V and -0.09 V. VREFP and static SEL highs track VDD, and VCM tracks VDD/2. The signed effect on each settled code voltage was not measured separately.

Per corner, per leg: `vdiff(code) = v(TOP_P) - v(TOP_N)` at t=500 ns after the reset-to-code event; ideal = `vdd*(2*code-511)/512` computed independently in `run_pex.py`; slope = `(vdiff(511)-vdiff(0))/511`, the measured differential step per P-side code (2x the ratified differential LSB `2*vdd/2^10`, mirrored differential drive). Headline: worst schematic-vs-extracted `vdiff` move across the grid is **60700.0 uV = 15.6962 LSB** at `tt/vcm=0.990_vdd=1.980V/27C` (ratified differential LSB at that rail); the endpoints' slope moves by at most **3.1181%** between legs.

`pex.json` reports a `count_only` model mismatch. It counts 20 reference and 1024 extracted MiM capacitor instances. On each side the reference represents weights 1 through 256 plus one termination with multipliers (512 unit capacitors). The layout draws those units individually.

Three features of `cdac_array.pex-extract.spice` explain the transfer deltas.

1. **Slope term -- `CTOP_P`/`CTOP_N`, the top-plate trunk's C to the substrate net** (136.61 fF on TOP_P here) scales the 512*C_u charge divider: 4436 fF of array capacitance against that ground term predicts a first-order slope shift of **2.99%**, to be read beside the measured per-corner slope columns above. The `Ccc__*` coupling caps between the top trunk and the weighted bottom-plate trunks (the coupled column of the table above) partially track the stepping bottom plates and pull the other way, which is why measured slope shifts sit near, not exactly on, the first-order figure.
2. **Common-mode term -- the floating substrate net's own motion** (klayout-tools#7066): v(vsubs) = 0.8005 V at tt at the read instant; each top plate carries 2.98% of that through its own C-to-vsubs -- the same arithmetic that leaves a stationary (code-constant) top plate offset in the extracted leg where the schematic leg sits exactly at its reset value. This term is an artifact of the missing substrate tie, not layout parasitics, and is disclosed as such.
3. **Relaxation/resistance -- the `R_*_t*` stitching resistors** on the top-plate and bottom-plate trunk fragments (210 ohm on each top trunk here) set how fast each event-driven redistribution settles; both legs are read at 500 ns, well past the last motion (the stationary top plate is bit-stable from ~100 ns at tt), so none of the rows above are unsettled transients.

Cross-validation of the schematic leg: `report.json.gz`: 20 schematic-leg values compared at tt/27C/1.80V and tt/27C/1.62V; 12/20 agree within 5 uV (worst |diff| 0.250 mV on `vtop_p_511` at `tt/vcm=0.810_vdd=1.620V/27C`). This PEX run used klt 0.9.0. At 1.62 V the comparison includes differences in simulator environment and in the SEL/reset stimulus and switch threshold. The observed residual measures their combined effect on the schematic leg.
